Abstract

Existing multi-view spatial reasoning remains constrained by sparse and fixed viewpoints. Consequently, vision-language models (VLMs) must infer spatial relations solely from these fixed views, leading to fragile cross-view alignment and a geometry-to-language bottleneck. To overcome these issues, we formulate a novel Seek-and-View reasoning paradigm that seeks a viewpoint to make the queried spatial relation directly observable and interprets the sought view to support the spatial reasoning. Building on this paradigm, we propose Vantage, a training-free and model-agnostic framework that structures VLM reasoning for viewpoint planning and evidence-augmented spatial understanding, with a 3D foundation model supporting view synthesis. Specifically, we develop a viewpoint-grounded reasoning stage that translates spatial queries into targeted viewpoint plans. A subsequent geometry-grounded evidence augmentation stage synthesizes new views and incorporates visual evidence alongside preceding reasoning contexts for final answering. Together, these stages reduce reliance on language-based cross-view alignment. Extensive experiments on six VLMs across six benchmarks show consistent improvements in overall accuracy, with an average absolute gain of 8.2% without fine-tuning.

Motivation

What still limits multi-view spatial reasoning?

Fragile cross-view alignment

Strong semantic and contextual reasoning ability does not inherently enforce the structured, globally consistent geometric constraints required across views, leaving the alignment implicit and error-prone.

Geometry-to-language bottleneck

Upon compressing the spatial geometry into discrete semantic descriptions, the reasoning would potentially overlook fine-grained spatial information.

The usual paradigm

View-and-Reason

Reasoning stays passive over the fixed, sparse observations.

  • Verbalized geometry, such as cognitive maps, scene graphs, or bounding boxes
  • Injected geometric priors, such as 3D positional encoding, feature fusion, or reconstructive supervision
Ours

Seek-and-View

Reasoning seeks a question-relevant view that makes the spatial evidence directly observable.

  • Question-relevant, evidence-seeking, and reasoning-oriented visual evidence acquisition
  • Spatial cues grounded in scene geometry and presented directly as visual evidence

Humans can conceptualize the 3D structure of a scene and seek a vantage viewpoint that resolves the spatial relation. Vantage gives a VLM the same option.

Method

Overview of the Vantage pipeline

Vantage is a training-free and model-agnostic framework that realizes Seek-and-View through viewpoint-grounded reasoning and geometry-grounded evidence augmentation. It structures VLM reasoning to plan question-relevant viewpoints and interpret the resulting visual evidence, with a 3D foundation model supporting view synthesis.

Viewpoint-grounded reasoning

The VLM analyzes the question and identifies the spatial evidence needed. It selects a reference view, plans a relative camera action from 17 predefined motions with three magnitude levels, and generates reasoning guidance for examining the sought view.

Geometry-grounded evidence augmentation

The backend converts the semantic action into an executable pose transformation in a gravity-aligned frame and synthesizes the requested view. Final VQA combines this view with the input observations, camera pose relations, question analysis, and reasoning guidance.

Consistent gains, no additional training

Across six benchmarks and six VLMs of varying scales, Vantage improves overall accuracy for every model, with an average absolute gain of 8.2% without fine-tuning.

Discussion

Where current VLMs + Seek-and-View still fail

Left Macro-averaged failure analysis. Right Representative failure cases by type.

We audit 1,200 incorrect predictions across four models and three multi-view benchmarks. When multiple errors occur, the first error in pipeline order determines the primary failure category. For smaller VLMs, incorrect analysis and incorrect view plan account for a substantial portion of failures, highlighting the difficulty of identifying the needed spatial evidence and translating it into an appropriate camera action. Unreliable synthesis is rare in the audited failures and mainly occurs under challenging visual conditions, suggesting that Seek-and-View can tolerate imperfect rendering when sufficient visual evidence is preserved. Reasoning failure occurs when the VLM under-utilizes useful evidence in the sought view or produces an inconsistent reasoning trace. Acquiring an informative view alone therefore does not guarantee a correct answer.
